## Preconditions and route selection

1. Verify PR #1158's actual merged implementation, the accepted follow-on source,
   final review/combined CI, and the actual runtime selected for adoption. A draft,
   open temporary merge SHA, publication or passing shared test is not adoption.
2. Identify the selected session's real account/profile, engine and placement from
   admission/custody, not its topic, current default account or a compatibility helper.
   Preserve original checkout, dirty files, provider history, queued inputs and
   unresolved allocations. Production adoption/deployment needs separate approval;
   staging remains stood down.
3. Reauthorize the current operator, account/catalog/model, permissions, workspace,
   target/runtime generation and original provider grant through their owners.
   Keep creating-login identity as provenance; a renewed login is not permission to
   switch account, provider, billing or workspace.
4. Observe the exact command and attempt, send acceptance, thread/turn/generation,
   tool results and completion/publication receipts. Read-only reconciliation does
   not replay the command. Lost acceptance, changed lineage/custody, unknown tools,
   pending hooks, cancellation or cleanup failure keeps work fenced.

Local native Codex uses its retained Local recovery/bootstrap/physical workspace
owners. OpenShell uses its own entry/admission, retained physical sandbox, grant,
lifecycle generation and provider-turn owners. Its local CLI exit does not prove
remote completion or cancellation. Neither route can copy the other's filesystem
assertions or transport-close policy. Exact completed provider metadata cannot settle
unknown downstream writes. A modern no-hook command also needs the witness retained
by its original accepted live owner before transport teardown. A reconstructed owner
cannot create that witness from provider metadata. Legacy sessions with missing
completion-phase applicability or effect evidence remain inspection-only; do not
backfill a receipt to make any historical session below resumable. Original late hook
success can settle only its original phase under fresh authority; it cannot
automatically release queued commands.

Claude SDK recovery relies on its exact native input/result and process/history
custody; Gemini relies on its native response/checkpoint and existing durable attempt
owner. Neither acquires Codex thread APIs or speculative provider repair calls.
Symposium recovery uses its original recipient claim/attempt registry and checkpoint
contract, including terminal conflicts and confirmed cleanup; ordinary chat recovery
is not a seat retry grant. Missing owner proof remains a precise blocked outcome.
